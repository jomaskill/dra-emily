# Feature Landscape

**Domain:** Brazilian facial-harmonization patient-acquisition website with WhatsApp consultation conversion
**Project:** Dra. Emily Beatriz — Website Conversion Review
**Market:** Brazilian Portuguese; Belo Horizonte and nearby patients
**Researched:** 2026-09-08
**Overall confidence:** MEDIUM

## Evidence Interpretation

This landscape separates evidence about patient decision-making from assumptions about this specific clinic:

- **Established evidence** means the feature is supported by multiple peer-reviewed studies, systematic reviews, authoritative clinical guidance, or official platform/regulatory guidance. It still requires Dra. Emily's clinical review and current Brazilian compliance review before publication.
- **Supported inference** means evidence identifies a patient need, but the proposed website treatment is a product-design inference rather than a directly tested conversion tactic.
- **Local hypothesis** means the feature is plausible for Belo Horizonte and the current site, but traffic, consultation quality, and patient research are not yet sufficient to claim it improves conversion.

The current evidence is mostly international rather than Brazil-specific. It supports a broad baseline—natural-looking outcomes, safety, comfort, individualized recommendations, adequate information, and aftercare matter—but does **not** justify selecting one audience or procedure as the site's flagship.

## Prospective Patient Decision Landscape

| Need or concern | Evidence-backed interpretation | Website implication |
|---|---|---|
| Look rested, younger, or more confident | Patients report appearance, psychosocial wellbeing, self-care, professional presentation, and prevention motives; motivations are often internally generated rather than imposed by a partner. | Use respectful, autonomy-preserving language such as “entender suas opções” and “preservar seus traços,” without promising confidence, happiness, career success, or life transformation. |
| Improve a specific concern without losing identity | Concern about unnatural-looking results appears alongside safety, cost, needles, and pain as a major barrier. | Explain subtle, individualized planning and what each procedure can and cannot change; avoid a standardized “ideal face.” |
| Know whether a treatment is appropriate | Clinical guidance places screening, medical history, goals, risk-benefit discussion, and individualized planning before treatment. | Sell the assessment, not a predefined treatment package. Clearly state that eligibility and the plan are determined in consultation. |
| Feel safe and know what happens if something goes wrong | Filler reviews and FDA guidance document common transient reactions and rare serious complications; follow-up and timely recognition matter. | Provide balanced risk, recovery, warning-sign, and follow-up information reviewed by the clinician; never call a procedure risk-free. |
| Reduce uncertainty about pain, downtime, timing, duration, and cost | Pain or needle fear and cost are reported barriers, while adequate information and aftercare contribute to satisfaction. | Give bounded, non-universal answers; state what varies and what will be confirmed in assessment. Explain how estimates are provided without using promotional pricing. |
| Trust the treating professional and clinic | Cosmetic-intervention guidance emphasizes competence, direct clinician involvement in consent, responsible marketing, and continuity of care. Brazilian dental rules require truthful identification and restrict titles, imagery, and promotional practices. | Make the treating professional, CRO registration, verified qualifications, location, care process, and limits more prominent than decorative luxury cues. |
| Start privately without committing to a procedure | WhatsApp officially supports visitor-initiated click-to-chat, but health data is sensitive and business messaging requires transparency and lawful handling. | Frame WhatsApp as an initial conversation, prefill only generic context, explain the next step, and keep symptoms, photos, identity, and medical details out of URLs and analytics. |

## Anti-Features

Features and tactics to deliberately avoid because they undermine informed choice, create compliance or privacy exposure, or generate misleading low-traffic conclusions.

| Anti-Feature | Why Avoid | What to Do Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Shame, defect, aging panic, or “perfect face” copy | Exploits appearance insecurity and can intensify psychological vulnerability. | Use neutral goal language, preserve identity, normalize doing nothing, and invite reflection. |
| Face scoring, “flaw detection,” selfie diagnosis, or AI outcome simulation | Converts subjective appearance into a false diagnosis and can create unrealistic expectations; simulated images are not guaranteed outcomes. | Offer a non-diagnostic goal/concern route to an individualized consultation. |
| Guaranteed naturalness, safety, duration, symmetry, confidence, or “100% results” | Outcomes, duration, reactions, and satisfaction vary; guarantees mislead and may create ethical/consumer-law exposure. | Use bounded, clinician-reviewed ranges and explain sources of variation. |
| Procedure bundles, automatic treatment recommendations, or universal candidacy | Encourages self-selection before history, anatomy, goals, and risks are assessed. | Sell assessment and document that the clinician may recommend a different plan or no intervention. |
| Countdown timers, scarcity, giveaways, or urgency pressure | Degrades reflective consent and can become misleading, coercive, or mercantilist even where a price or discount disclosure is otherwise permitted. | Use stable, factual CTAs with no artificial time pressure; submit any future campaign for current CRO-MG/legal review. |
| Price-led acquisition or hidden “starting at” bait | Price is a real barrier, but current Brazilian rules have changed and remain context-sensitive; a teaser price also cannot represent an individualized plan. | Explain when and how a personalized estimate is provided; keep prices out in this milestone, as the project requires, unless a later approved scope adds them after current compliance review. |
| Indiscriminate before-and-after gallery | Can imply repeatable outcomes, expose patients, and may be impermissible depending on publisher identity, consent, medium, and current CFO/CRO interpretation. | Default to process/education imagery; publish a case only after documented authorization, provenance, professional attribution, individualized-results context, and current compliance approval. |
| Filtered, retouched, mismatched-lighting, or unlabeled patient imagery | Manipulates apparent outcomes and prevents fair interpretation. | Use standardized, unretouched documentation only where publication is approved. |
| Procedure-in-progress or sensational injection video | Can trivialize risk, emphasize spectacle, and conflicts with CFO guidance against procedural-progress/sensational content. | Use clinician-reviewed diagrams or calm educational explanations. |
| Fabricated, stale, rounded-up, or unattributed ratings and testimonials | Creates false trust and can become misleading as counts change. | Link to a live, verifiable source or omit the claim; maintain review provenance and date. |
| Health details embedded in WhatsApp links or analytics | Prefilled text and URLs may be collected by browsers, platforms, logs, and analytics; health information is sensitive. | Send only controlled page/procedure/placement identifiers to analytics and keep the opening message generic. |
| Open-ended health intake form on the public marketing site | Expands LGPD, security, retention, and incident-response obligations beyond this milestone. | Conduct necessary history through an approved private clinical workflow after contact; do not add a patient portal or intake system here. |
| Bot that diagnoses, determines candidacy, or handles emergencies | A marketing automation cannot safely evaluate anatomy, history, complications, or urgent symptoms. | Use automation only for identity, hours, and routing; provide a clear human and urgent-care escalation path. |
| Unconsented analytics, hidden trackers, or health-based remarketing audiences | Creates privacy risk and can reveal or infer sensitive healthcare interest. | Use data minimization, transparent consent/privacy controls, aggregate measurement, and no sensitive ad personalization. |
| Premature A/B or multivariate testing | Sparse conversions produce slow, unstable, or misleading winners and waste qualified traffic. | Start with instrumentation, usability sessions, interviews, and sequential high-impact releases; require an explicit sample-size/power plan before controlled experiments. |
| Declaring a flagship procedure or narrow persona from current traffic | The existing data is insufficient to show demand, lead quality, eligibility, or revenue by procedure. | Keep broad natural-results/safety positioning and let measured qualified consultations determine later prominence. |
| Keyword-stuffed “Belo Horizonte” copy or duplicate neighborhood doorway pages | Damages readability and creates weak local content without improving real-world trust. | Use one accurate location story, useful visit details, verified Business Profile data, and natural Brazilian Portuguese. |
| English-first clinical jargon or imported luxury-clinic tone | Creates distance and may not match how local patients describe their questions. | Use clear Brazilian Portuguese, familiar search terms such as “botox” alongside “toxina botulínica,” and test wording with local prospects. |

## Low-Traffic Validation Plan

The site should not label hypotheses as “high converting” until it can observe appropriate consultations, not just clicks.

### Baseline measurement

1. Define `whatsapp_consultation_click` and validate it in GA4 DebugView.
2. Use controlled, non-sensitive parameters: `source_page`, `procedure`, `cta_placement`, and optionally `message_variant` as an internal code—not the message text.
3. Record page views, CTA clicks, and a privacy-preserving manual outcome tally for legitimate inquiry, consultation scheduled, attended, and eligible. Do not join GA4 to named patients.
4. Annotate release dates and exclude staff/test traffic where practical.
5. Review counts and rates together; at low volume, one or two leads can swing percentages materially.

### Qualitative validation before experiments

Run small rounds—typically about five participants per round, with more when segments differ—of moderated mobile-first tasks with adults in the Belo Horizonte area who are considering or could plausibly consider facial aesthetic treatment. Include first-time and safety-conscious participants.

Tasks should test whether a visitor can:

- explain who Dra. Emily is and verify her professional registration;
- understand that consultation precedes treatment selection;
- find what a procedure may and may not do;
- find meaningful risks, recovery expectations, and follow-up arrangements;
- locate the clinic and determine whether visiting is practical;
- start a WhatsApp conversation without disclosing sensitive information;
- distinguish factual information from a promise or guarantee.

### Hypotheses to validate

| Hypothesis | Why plausible | Earliest useful signal | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Avaliação individualizada” converts better than procedure-purchase language | Fits clinical journey and uncertainty about the right treatment. | Task comprehension; WhatsApp click-to-legitimate-inquiry ratio. | Keep only if people understand the next step and conversation quality does not decline. |
| Naturalness and safety proof near the first CTA reduces hesitation | Unnatural results and safety are documented barriers. | Usability confidence; objection themes in WhatsApp; CTA engagement. | Promote if it resolves questions without being interpreted as a guarantee. |
| A “não sei qual procedimento” route captures qualified first-time patients | Visitors may know the concern but not the product. | Route usage; legitimate inquiry and scheduling share. | Keep if it produces assessable conversations rather than vague or unsafe self-diagnosis. |
| A mobile persistent CTA improves action without harming comprehension | WhatsApp is the primary mobile conversion. | Misclicks, task completion, CTA placement counts, accessibility review. | Keep only if it does not obscure safety content, controls, or reading. |
| Exact neighborhood and visit logistics increase local trust | Local ranking and visit decisions depend on accurate proximity information. | Location-section engagement; fewer logistics questions; scheduled consultations. | Expand only with verified operational facts. |
| Procedure-specific prefill improves handoff quality | Context can reduce blank-chat friction. | Staff-rated conversation clarity and reply completion. | Keep generic; retire any variant that encourages health detail in the URL or first message. |

### Experimentation gate

Do not run a conventional A/B or multivariate test until there is a written primary metric, baseline rate, minimum detectable effect, required sample size, test duration, and stopping rule. Before that threshold, use qualitative findings, funnel diagnostics, and dated sequential releases; report changes as directional, not causal.

## MVP Recommendation

Prioritize:

1. **Trust and compliance baseline:** verify professional identity, qualifications, clinic facts, clinical claims, patient-image/testimonial rights, and remove guarantees or unsupported claims.
2. **Consultation-first information architecture:** make individualized assessment, realistic expectations, safety, and naturalness the core story across home and procedure pages.
3. **Patient information baseline:** add consistent “what it may/may not do,” risk/recovery, candidacy boundary, aftercare, follow-up, and objection-focused FAQ content.
4. **WhatsApp journey:** standardize the primary CTA, generic context-aware prefill, response expectations, privacy notice, and desktop/mobile behavior.
5. **Local trust:** reconcile address, neighborhood, hours, contact, map, and Google Business Profile identity for Belo Horizonte intent.
6. **Measurement baseline:** instrument named WhatsApp events with controlled non-sensitive parameters and create a manual qualified-conversation outcome process.
7. **Qualitative verification:** test the complete mobile and desktop task journey with relevant local prospects before conversion experiments.

Defer:

- **Flagship-procedure promotion:** insufficient traffic and consultation-quality evidence.
- **Classic A/B or multivariate tests:** insufficient volume until a sample-size calculation says otherwise.
- **Automated procedure recommender or AI simulation:** clinical, ethical, expectation, and privacy risks outweigh acquisition value.
- **Public intake/CRM/patient portal:** expands data-handling scope and is outside this milestone.
- **Online scheduling:** the agreed conversion remains a WhatsApp consultation.
- **Price, discount, or promotion features:** excluded pending a specific business need and current dental-compliance review.
- **Before-and-after expansion:** blocked until provenance, consent, publisher identity, current CFO/CRO-MG applicability, and standardized presentation are verified.

## Research Gaps

- No Brazil- or Belo Horizonte-specific study located establishes which motivation, age cohort, or procedure should dominate this clinic's acquisition strategy.
- Current interpretation of CFO/CRO-MG rules for a personally branded website, testimonials, before-and-after imagery, and specialty language must be confirmed at implementation time, especially given ongoing regulatory/legal uncertainty.
- Public sources cannot verify Dra. Emily's current CRO registration, specialty registrations, clinic service levels, image consents, ratings, response times, or follow-up operations; each is a release gate, not an assumption.
- The effect of specific Portuguese CTA wording, sticky CTA placement, local logistics, and procedure ordering is unknown until qualitative testing and attributable qualified consultations produce evidence.
- Clicks alone do not measure clinical appropriateness or business value; the clinic needs a privacy-preserving downstream outcome process before optimizing traffic allocation.
